# Lendhous

GT's private lending platform, built from Product Spec v3 (`product-spec-v3.pdf`, kept outside this repo). It runs on the same stack and infrastructure as UPB Capital (`../upb-capital-source`): Next.js 15, React 19, TypeScript, Tailwind 4 and shadcn, Prisma on MongoDB, signed-cookie sessions, R2 or Vercel Blob for files, and Resend or SMTP for mail. The look follows the LendHous brand board: cream `#FAFBF8`, dark green `#202B28` and sage `#677D67`, Georgia for headings and Verdana for text, with near-square corners from the marketing site. The tokens are in `app/globals.css`; the browser and home-screen icons (`app/icon.png`, `app/apple-icon.png`) are the brand's "L" mark.

Three layers live here today:

- **The public site.** The landing page at `/`, plus loan programs, investor guides, calculators, the quote request, about and disclosures, under `app/(site)`. No page needs an account. It was rebuilt from the LendHous marketing site's export, on the brand kit's colors and fonts. Its styles are in `app/(site)/site.css`, scoped under `.site` so they never reach the panel. The quote request saves each request, alerts the team (every owner-admin, plus `TEAM_EMAIL`), and lists it for staff at `/admin/leads`.
- **The panel.** Portal login on the site opens `/login`, and signing in opens the panel under `/admin`: the staff pipeline at `/admin`, `/admin/capital`, `/admin/deals`, `/admin/leads`, `/admin/loan-sales`, `/admin/draws`, `/admin/servicing`, `/admin/members` and `/admin/audit`, then `/admin/borrower`, `/admin/apply`, `/admin/partner`, `/admin/buyer`, `/admin/broker` and `/admin/account`. Also `/admin/new-loan`, `/admin/partners` and `/admin/settings`. Every page needs a signed-in account, and the account's role decides which section it opens. Real accounts work on the real loan book in the database: loans, documents, draws, payments, bids, sales and messages. The server checks every change against the person's role and sends each person only what they may see (`lib/book-access.ts`). Demo seats (`@lendhous.test`) get a shared book of FAKE demo records in the browser, and any account with no loans can load the same FAKE book for itself with **Load sample data**, until an owner turns that off in Settings. The rates in `lib/pricing-config.ts` are still placeholders; the owner replaces them at `/admin/pricing` (below).
- **Applying for a loan.** A borrower, a broker for a client, or staff from a phone call fills in one application at `/admin/apply`: the project and numbers with a live estimate, then the borrowing entity and its guarantors, past deals from the last 36 months, and seven credit questions. The loan is priced on the lowest guarantor's score and on the deals listed, not on numbers typed in, and its document list follows from the answers, each item saying why it's needed. Staff starting a loan without these leave them for the borrower, who is asked on their loan page. Before a term sheet can go out, someone with term sheet authority signs off the Credit review tab, where processors tick off each past deal against its settlement statement; signing off reprices the loan on the verified deals only.
- **The photo link.** `/photos/<token>` is the one page outside the public site that holds real records, and it needs no account. Staff or a borrower send a contractor or borrower a link from a draw or a loan's Documents tab. It walks them through the property and each budget line, saying what to photograph, and the photos appear on the draw by budget line. `/photos/demo` is a preview that sends nothing.
- **Servicing.** Staff record each payment received on a loan with its amount, date, method and reference, at `/admin/servicing` or on the loan's Servicing tab, and see where the money will go before saving it: the oldest payment due first, then late fees, then payments ahead of time, with anything smaller than a whole payment held as a credit. A nightly job (`servicing` in `lib/jobs.ts`) marks a payment late once its grace days pass and charges its late fee, moves a loan to delinquent at 30 days past due and to matured the day after maturity, and emails the borrower and the team: late notices, 30 and 90 days past due, maturity 60 and 30 days ahead, and matured. Payoffs add unpaid late fees and the default rate for days past maturity. Payments can be reversed and late fees waived, each with a reason, and the ledger is worked out again from what's stored, so the figures stay right either way. The grace days, late fee, delinquency and default rate are placeholders in `lib/pricing-config.ts` until the attorney sets them against the note.
- **Draw protections under Florida's construction lien law (Fla. Stat. ch. 713).** Each rehab or construction loan keeps a lien file on its Closing tab: the contractor; the Notice of Commencement, checked to name LendHous as lender and to receive copies of notices to owner, to be recorded after the mortgage (or confirmed first on the same day), to have work start within 90 days, and to be within its end date (a year unless it states another); the notices to owner subcontractors and suppliers serve; and the builder's risk or hazard policy. Every draw then needs a statutory lien waiver (713.20) from the contractor and from every lienor whose notice came in before it, checked by the team for the amount, the date it covers work through (never after today), and whether it's conditional on the check clearing. The last draw, marked or the one that uses up the holdback, takes final waivers and the contractor's final payment affidavit (713.06(3)(d)), and is held if the affidavit lists unpaid lienors. The borrower sees what each draw needs and uploads waivers from their loan page; the team can upload for them or send one back with what's wrong. A draw isn't released while anything is missing, the notice has a problem, or the policy has lapsed, unless someone with the new `override_draw_holds` permission (owners by default) releases it past the holds standing then, with a reason that's logged and sent to the team. A nightly `insurance` job warns the borrower and the team 30 days before a policy ends and again when it lapses (`lib/lien-law.ts`, `components/loan/LienProtection.tsx`, `components/loan/DrawLienChecklist.tsx`). The 30 days and which waivers count as conditional are placeholders until GT and the attorney set them.
- **How a loan ends, or gets more time.** Before funding, someone with term sheet authority can decline an application, and the borrower, or the team on their word, can withdraw it, each with a reason. Its capital and open bids are let go, it leaves the active pipeline, and the borrower and broker are told it was declined but not why: the reasons go in the attorney-approved notice. The team can reopen it. After funding, the owner can declare a default (payments, maturity, insurance, taxes or a covenant) with an effective date, which starts default interest and stops draws, and lift it once nothing is owed. A borrower can ask for an extension from 60 days before maturity and the team at any time; two people with extension authority approve it, and once the agreement is signed and the fee is in, the maturity moves and a matured or defaulted loan is current again. A payoff is recorded with the money received and refused if it's short of the payoff that day, unless it was paid on a payoff letter (below); it closes the loan, and the satisfaction of mortgage is tracked from sent to recorded against Florida's 60 days. The rules are in `lib/loan-exits.ts`; the extension limits and fee are placeholders in `lib/pricing-config.ts`.
- **Decline notices (Regulation B, 12 CFR 1002.9 and 1002.12).** Staff mark when an application became complete (signing off the credit review marks it if nobody has) and record the business's gross revenue for its last fiscal year on the Credit review tab, or in the decline form. A decline starts the notice: with $1 million or less in revenue, or revenue unknown, it's due 30 days from the completed application (or from the decision on an incomplete one) and gives the specific reasons; over $1 million it's due in a reasonable time and tells them how to ask for the reasons in writing within 60 days, and a logged request makes the statement of reasons due 30 days later. The decline reason starts as a specific reason that staff edit, at most four, and "internal standards" or "didn't qualify" is refused. The letter is a PDF with the action taken, the reasons, Reg B's model ECOA notice with the federal agency, and LendHous's address and phone from Settings, which it can't go without; it's emailed to the borrower's sign-in address or marked mailed, and the borrower can open it from their loan page. The team hears when one is due, sees it in the menu and in the morning email when five days or fewer remain, and the loan shows how long its records have to be kept (12 months from the notice; 60 days over $1 million unless they asked). An owner can place a legal hold, which keeps them until it's lifted. Reopening a declined application before its notice went out drops the notice (`lib/adverse-action.ts`, `components/loan/AdverseAction.tsx`). The letter's wording, the reason texts, the federal agency and the "reasonable time" of 30 days are placeholders until the attorney sets them.
- **Payoff letters (Fla. Stat. 701.04).** The borrower asks in the portal; a title company, attorney or anyone else the borrower authorizes asks at `/payoff` with no account, attaching the borrower's signed authorization; staff log requests that came by email, fax, mail or courier with the day they were sent. Each request gets the day it counts as received (mail five business days after the postmark, anything else the day sent, a weekend or holiday moving to the next business day) and the letter's due date 10 days later, shown on `/admin/servicing`, counted in the menu and in a morning email to the team when two days or fewer remain. The borrower is told whenever someone else asks. Staff check the authorization (or, when a borrower writes from an address that isn't their sign-in, call them) before the letter can go. The letter itemizes principal, interest at the note rate, default interest, late fees and any credit held, with the total good through a date and the amount per day after; it has no wire instructions and nothing that qualifies its figures, which the statute voids. It's emailed as a PDF with a link that always opens the newest letter (`/payoff-letter/<token>`, only its hash is kept), and how sending went is kept on the letter. When a payment, reversal, fee, draw or default moves the figures, the letter is flagged and the team alerted at once, because a correction only takes the old letter's place if it arrives by 3 p.m. at least one business day before payment; the corrected letter goes the same way, with a copy to the borrower when someone else asked. A payoff paid on a letter good through that day is taken as the payoff even if the letter came up short: the mortgage is released and the rest stays owed. `/payoff-letter/demo` is a sample (`lib/payoffs.ts`, `lib/payoff-pdf.ts`, `lib/payoff-delivery.ts`). How far ahead a letter may be good through (30 days), which holidays count (the Federal Reserve's) and the letter's wording are placeholders until GT and the attorney set them.
- **The quote request.** The public site's `/start` page is a loan quote request a borrower or a broker can send. It covers the borrower (or the broker and their client), citizenship, credit band and experience; the property's address, with Google's suggestions when `GOOGLE_MAPS_API_KEY` is set, and its type; and the loan type, purpose, exit, numbers including the ARV, up to three comps supporting the ARV, and a funding date. The visitor sees indicative terms worked out by the same engine on the pricing the owner last published, with a clear "estimate, not an offer" note. The team gets a lead with the whole request sealed, and the leads inbox shows the address, ARV, credit band and comps (`lib/quote.ts`). `/programs` compares the four programs from the live pricing, so the advertised rates and leverage can't drift from what loans actually get (`lib/program-compare.ts`).
- **The borrower application link.** From a lead, or a loan still in review, a loan officer sends the borrower a secure link (`/apply/<token>`, no sign-in) to a five-step application. The steps are loan and contact, the property (with flood zone), the loan details, vesting and guarantors (each with date of birth, Social Security number, home address, marital status, citizenship, ownership and the declarations), and review and sign. It opens filled in with what's known, saves as they go, picks up where they left off, and works for 14 days; sending a new one turns the old one off. Signing takes four consents (placeholder wording until the attorney approves it) and the typed full legal name; the signature is kept with the time, IP address, consent version and a digest of what was signed. For a lead it starts the loan and invites the borrower to the portal; for a loan in review it updates the numbers and profile, keeping the loan's pricing. Birth dates, Social Security numbers, home addresses and the EIN stay sealed with the signed application and never enter the loan book. The loan's Credit review tab shows the application with numbers masked; revealing one takes `reveal_sensitive` and is logged (`lib/borrower-application.ts`, `lib/application-links.ts`). `/apply/demo` is a preview that sends nothing.
- **Property photos.** A borrower or contractor can send photos of the property, up to 12 per request or loan: with the quote request (the browser gets a key for two hours after sending it), on the property step of the application link (each sent as it's added, and they can take one back), and from their loan page, where the broker and the team can add them too. Each is shrunk on the phone first, checked to be a picture, and stored encrypted like every loan file. The team sees a lead's photos in the leads inbox; when the lead becomes a loan, by staff or by signing the application link, the photos move to the loan and show on the loan's Documents tab and on the borrower's and broker's loan pages. Only the team can remove one, and a partner's blind teaser never shows them (`lib/property-photos.ts`, `components/PropertyPhotoPicker.tsx`, `components/loan/PropertyPhotos.tsx`).
- **Self sign-up.** Anyone can create a borrower account at `/login/signup`, linked from the login page and from the quote request's reply. The account can't sign in until they open the link emailed to them, which confirms the address and sets the password; the team is told when that happens. Signing up with an address that already has an account changes nothing and emails that account a password link instead, so the page never says which addresses are taken. It's rate limited, has a honeypot, and the reply takes the same time either way. Once signed in, they apply from their portal and add photos to the loan; a loan staff start for the same email shows up there too. An owner turns sign-ups off in Settings to take borrowers by invitation only; the team sees unconfirmed sign-ups on the Members page as "Email not confirmed" (`lib/members.ts`, `actions/signup.ts`, `app/login/signup`).
- **Motion.** Steps in the application link and the portal application rise and settle with Framer Motion, and forms that open under a button glide open. Slide-in panels use the same easing. Motion is short and vertical, so nothing passes a phone's edge mid-move, and it calms down for anyone who asks their device for less motion (`components/motion.tsx`).
- **Conditions (Spec A10, B17, A23).** Separate from the documents, each condition has a category (before the loan documents, before funding, or after closing), an owner (borrower, processor, underwriter or title company), a due date, and a status: open, submitted, cleared or waived. When a loan starts clearing conditions it gets the library's active standard conditions for its loan type, and the borrower and broker are told what's theirs. Staff add more from the library or write one for that loan on the loan's Conditions tab (from underwriting on; a funded loan takes only after-closing ones, and conditions set before a term sheet are listed on it). The borrower and broker see only the borrower's conditions, with what's needed, when it's due and an upload button; a file marks one submitted and tells the team. Anyone with `manage_conditions` clears a condition or sends a file back with what's wrong (the borrower sees why); waiving takes the new `waive_conditions` permission (owners and underwriters by default) and a reason. A loan can't clear to close until every condition before closing is cleared or waived. Owners edit the standard conditions on the Conditions library page under Settings: each change is a new version in the audit log, and a retired one can be brought back. The starting library is a placeholder until GT and the attorney set the real list (`lib/conditions.ts`, `components/loan/ConditionsPanel.tsx`, `components/ConditionLibrary.tsx`).
- **Closing and funding.** From an accepted term sheet, the loan's Closing tab records the title company or closing attorney, the title company's checklist (title commitment, closing protection letter and settlement statement before clear to close, signed loan documents before funding, the recorded mortgage after), and the wire instructions funding goes to. Instructions are entered in the portal only, never from an email, and the routing number's check digit is tested. Someone other than whoever entered them confirms them by calling a number found independently (the website, an earlier closing, a license lookup), and the call is logged. A loan can't be approved for funding, or funded, until that's done. New instructions replace the old ones, clear every funding approval, need a new call-back, and alert the team. Borrowers, brokers and partners never receive wire instructions (`lib/closing.ts`).
- **The title company's link.** `/closing/<token>` needs no account. Staff who handle conditions or funding send it from the Closing tab once the closing agent is saved; it is emailed to the closer, shown once to copy, works for 30 days, and sending a new one or turning it off stops the old one. Through it the title company uploads the closing checklist and nothing else on the loan, enters its own escrow wire instructions (which still need the call-back above, and a change clears every funding approval), and confirms the funds reached escrow and the mortgage's recording, which alerts the team and the borrower. It sees the vesting names, the property, the loan amount and closing date, and whether instructions are on file with the account masked, never credit, payments or other documents. It stops working when the loan is declined, withdrawn or paid off. `/closing/demo` is a preview that sends nothing (`lib/title-links.ts`).
- **Sanctions screening (OFAC).** The borrowing company, the borrower and each guarantor are screened against Treasury's SDN list and its aliases when an application arrives or its guarantors change, and every night after. The nightly `sanctions` job downloads the list from Treasury and keeps the old one if a download fails. Names are compared regardless of word order, accents and company-type words, so a close spelling is caught but one shared surname isn't. A possible match holds the application until someone with `clear_ofac` reviews it, recording what they compared; a confirmed match stops all money on the loan and tells the team to report it. An application can't go to underwriting without a clean screening, and a loan can't fund or release a draw on one older than three days (`lib/ofac.ts`, `lib/sanctions-store.ts`).
- **Term sheets (Spec A9, B16).** Once a loan is in underwriting and its credit review is signed off, the underwriter builds the term sheet on the loan's Term sheet tab. It starts from the matrix on the loan's own pricing version, and they set the rate, points, term and prepayment terms, seeing the loan amount, payment, cash to close and all-in cost as they go. The underwriter's dollar limit applies. A rate or points outside the matrix's range for that loan, an all-in cost at or over the ceiling, or a rate over 18% on a loan of $500,000 or less (Florida's usury cap) needs an exception: the underwriter says why, the owners are told, and an owner other than whoever asked approves or declines it, which is recorded on the term sheet. Nothing can go above 25% (criminal usury). Each term sheet goes out as a numbered version, emailed to the borrower and broker, with a PDF made on the server, and is open for 7 days. The borrower sees only the newest one, without the matrix or the exception, and accepts it by typing their name, asks for changes (back to underwriting, with their note), or declines it (which withdraws the application). Accepting locks the rate and points: later number edits and pricing changes don't move them. A nightly `termSheets` job reminds the borrower and broker two days before one expires, and expires it the day after its last day, sending the loan back to underwriting. Staff see every version and open any version's PDF (`lib/term-sheets.ts`, `lib/term-sheet-pdf.ts`, `components/loan/TermSheetBuilder.tsx`). The 7 days, the prepayment options and the term sheet's wording are placeholders until GT and the attorney set them.
- **Pricing you can change safely.** Every loan is priced on a pricing version and keeps it: its rate, points, leverage, late fee, default rate and extension terms come from the version it was quoted on, so publishing new numbers never moves a loan already priced. The owner edits rates, points, leverage, minimum scores, terms, loan sizes, fees and the note's late-payment and extension terms at `/admin/pricing`. They see what the change does to each application still in review before publishing it with a reason, and every version is kept and audited. Rates above 25% a year (criminal usury in Florida) can't be published, and anything above 18% is flagged for the attorney. New applications get the current version; staff can move an application still in review onto it with **Reprice on current pricing**. Loans saved before versions existed stay on the original numbers in `lib/pricing-config.ts` (`lib/pricing-versions.ts`, `lib/pricing-store.ts`).
- **The backend.** Sign-in, roles and permissions, encryption at rest, dual control, audit, storage, mail, jobs, health, and the release guard. The loan book is saved as JSON records (`BookRecord`), one per loan, bid, draw and so on, with the rules in `lib/book.ts`. Splitting those records into their own tables is still open, for when reporting across them needs it.

## Run

You need Node 22+ and a MongoDB replica set: Prisma needs one for transactions. Use either:

```bash
docker compose up -d          # mongo:7, one-node replica set (same as UPB)
npm run db:dev                # or: the same without Docker, data in .mongo-data/ (Ctrl-C to stop)
```

Then:

```bash
cp .env.example .env          # local defaults work as is; set CRON_SECRET to try /api/cron
npm install                   # postinstall runs prisma generate
npm run db:seed               # drops the local database, syncs validators and indexes, adds the demo seats
npm run dev                   # http://localhost:3000 is the public site; Portal login opens /login
```

`npm run db:indexes` syncs the MongoDB validators and indexes. It is safe to run again; the seed and release builds both run it. There are no migrations. As in UPB, never use `prisma db push`.

## What is encrypted

With `FIELD_ENCRYPTION_KEYS`, before anything is stored:

- every loan book record: names, addresses, credit scores, liquidity, messages, draw contacts and photo requests
- a website brief's name, email, phone, city, state and notes
- saved alerts in the notifications inbox
- every stored file and its name, so the bucket only ever holds ciphertext

Sign-in email addresses, account names and phone numbers are not, because sign-in and alerts look people up by them. The audit log holds who did what and from where, not the details. Passwords are hashed, and password, photo, application, title and payoff links are stored only as hashes. Files always come through the signed-in route rather than a signed link to the bucket, since the bucket can't decrypt them.

Records saved before this still read. `npm run db:encrypt` and the daily job encrypt them, and move records to the newest key after a rotation. To rotate, put the new key first (`k2:...,k1:...`), run `db:encrypt`, and only then remove the old key.

## MongoDB instead of PostgreSQL

Spec v3 assumes PostgreSQL. Lendhous uses MongoDB to match UPB, and covers the gaps this way:

- **No row-level security.** Every server action checks `can()` from `lib/permissions.ts`, and from B1 every loan query goes through one data layer scoped to the viewer.
- **No CHECK constraints.** Collection validators in `lib/mongo-validators.ts`, applied by `db:indexes`, reject rule-breaking writes at the database. Dual approval is the first one.
- **No migrations.** Schema changes need a backfill script under `scripts/` when existing documents must change.
- **32-bit `Int`.** Money is integer cents. Loan-level amounts fit (cap $21.4M). Capital totals will use `BigInt` when they arrive (B3).
- **Append-only ledgers (B4).** An Atlas custom role without update or delete rights on the ledger collections, used through a second connection.
