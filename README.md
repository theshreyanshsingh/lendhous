# Lendhous

GT's private lending platform, built from Product Spec v3 (`product-spec-v3.pdf`, kept outside this repo). It runs on the same stack and infrastructure as UPB Capital (`../upb-capital-source`): Next.js 15, React 19, TypeScript, Tailwind 4 and shadcn, Prisma on MongoDB, signed-cookie sessions, R2 or Vercel Blob for files, and Resend or SMTP for mail. The look follows the LendHous brand board: cream `#FAFBF8`, dark green `#202B28` and sage `#677D67`, Georgia for headings and Verdana for text, with near-square corners from the marketing site. The tokens are in `app/globals.css`; the browser and home-screen icons (`app/icon.png`, `app/apple-icon.png`) are the brand's "L" mark.

Three layers live here today:

- **The public site.** The landing page at `/`, plus loan programs, investor guides, calculators, the quote request, about and disclosures, under `app/(site)`. No page needs an account. It was rebuilt from the LendHous marketing site's export, on the brand kit's colors and fonts. Its styles are in `app/(site)/site.css`, scoped under `.site` so they never reach the panel. The quote request saves each request, alerts the team (every owner-admin, plus `TEAM_EMAIL`), and lists it for staff at `/admin/leads`.
- **The panel.** Portal login on the site opens `/login`, and signing in opens the panel under `/admin`: the staff pipeline at `/admin`, `/admin/capital`, `/admin/deals`, `/admin/leads`, `/admin/loan-sales`, `/admin/draws`, `/admin/servicing`, `/admin/members` and `/admin/audit`, then `/admin/borrower`, `/admin/apply`, `/admin/partner`, `/admin/buyer`, `/admin/broker` and `/admin/account`. Also `/admin/new-loan`, `/admin/partners` and `/admin/settings`. Every page needs a signed-in account, and the account's role decides which section it opens. Real accounts work on the real loan book in the database: loans, documents, draws, payments, bids, sales and messages. The server checks every change against the person's role and sends each person only what they may see (`lib/book-access.ts`). Demo seats (`@lendhous.test`) get a shared book of FAKE demo records in the browser, and any account with no loans can load the same FAKE book for itself with **Load sample data**, until an owner turns that off in Settings. The rates in `lib/pricing-config.ts` are still placeholders; the owner replaces them at `/admin/pricing` (below).
- **Applying for a loan.** A borrower signed in to the portal applies at `/admin/apply` with the same five-step application the secure link uses (below): it starts with what their account says, saves on the server as they go and picks up where they left off for 90 days after the last save, shows the live estimate the loan will be priced on and what will be asked for, and ends with the four consents and a typed signature. Signing starts the loan as theirs, and the signed copy, with Social Security numbers and birth dates sealed outside the loan book, sits on the loan's Credit review tab like a link's. Demo accounts and sample data keep theirs in the browser. A broker for a client, or staff from a phone call, use the broker and staff form at `/admin/broker/new` and `/admin/new-loan`: the project and numbers with a live estimate, then the borrowing entity and its guarantors, past deals from the last 36 months, and seven credit questions. The loan is priced on the lowest guarantor's score and on the deals listed, not on numbers typed in, and its document list follows from the answers, each item saying why it's needed. Staff starting a loan without these leave them for the borrower, who is asked on their loan page. Before a term sheet can go out, someone with term sheet authority signs off the Credit review tab, where processors tick off each past deal against its settlement statement; signing off reprices the loan on the verified deals only.
- **The photo link.** `/photos/<token>` is the one page outside the public site that holds real records, and it needs no account. Staff or a borrower send a contractor or borrower a link from a draw or a loan's Documents tab. It walks them through the property and each budget line, saying what to photograph, and the photos appear on the draw by budget line. `/photos/demo` is a preview that sends nothing.
- **Servicing.** Staff record each payment received on a loan with its amount, date, method and reference, at `/admin/servicing` or on the loan's Servicing tab, and see where the money will go before saving it: the oldest payment due first, then late fees, then payments ahead of time, with anything smaller than a whole payment held as a credit. A nightly job (`servicing` in `lib/jobs.ts`) marks a payment late once its grace days pass and charges its late fee, moves a loan to delinquent at 30 days past due and to matured the day after maturity, and emails the borrower and the team: late notices, 30 and 90 days past due, maturity 60 and 30 days ahead, and matured. Payoffs add unpaid late fees and the default rate for days past maturity. Payments can be reversed and late fees waived, each with a reason, and the ledger is worked out again from what's stored, so the figures stay right either way. The grace days, late fee, delinquency and default rate are placeholders in `lib/pricing-config.ts` until the attorney sets them against the note.
- **Draw protections under Florida's construction lien law (Fla. Stat. ch. 713).** Each rehab or construction loan keeps a lien file on its Closing tab: the contractor; the Notice of Commencement, checked to name LendHous as lender and to receive copies of notices to owner, to be recorded after the mortgage (or confirmed first on the same day), to have work start within 90 days, and to be within its end date (a year unless it states another); the notices to owner subcontractors and suppliers serve; and the builder's risk or hazard policy. Every draw then needs a statutory lien waiver (713.20) from the contractor and from every lienor whose notice came in before it, checked by the team for the amount, the date it covers work through (never after today), and whether it's conditional on the check clearing. The last draw, marked or the one that uses up the holdback, takes final waivers and the contractor's final payment affidavit (713.06(3)(d)), and is held if the affidavit lists unpaid lienors. The borrower sees what each draw needs and uploads waivers from their loan page; the team can upload for them or send one back with what's wrong. A draw isn't released while anything is missing, the notice has a problem, or the policy has lapsed, unless someone with the new `override_draw_holds` permission (owners by default) releases it past the holds standing then, with a reason that's logged and sent to the team. A nightly `insurance` job warns the borrower and the team 30 days before a policy ends and again when it lapses (`lib/lien-law.ts`, `components/loan/LienProtection.tsx`, `components/loan/DrawLienChecklist.tsx`). The 30 days and which waivers count as conditional are placeholders until GT and the attorney set them.
- **How a loan ends, or gets more time.** Before funding, someone with term sheet authority can decline an application, and the borrower, or the team on their word, can withdraw it, each with a reason. Its capital and open bids are let go, it leaves the active pipeline, and the borrower and broker are told it was declined but not why: the reasons go in the attorney-approved notice. The team can reopen it. After funding, the owner can declare a default (payments, maturity, insurance, taxes or a covenant) with an effective date, which starts default interest and stops draws, and lift it once nothing is owed. A borrower can ask for an extension from 60 days before maturity and the team at any time; two people with extension authority approve it, and once the agreement is signed and the fee is in, the maturity moves and a matured or defaulted loan is current again. A payoff is recorded with the money received and refused if it's short of the payoff that day, unless it was paid on a payoff letter (below); it closes the loan, and the satisfaction of mortgage is tracked from sent to recorded against Florida's 60 days. The rules are in `lib/loan-exits.ts`; the extension limits and fee are placeholders in `lib/pricing-config.ts`.
- **Decline notices (Regulation B, 12 CFR 1002.9 and 1002.12).** Staff mark when an application became complete (signing off the credit review marks it if nobody has) and record the business's gross revenue for its last fiscal year on the Credit review tab, or in the decline form. A decline starts the notice: with $1 million or less in revenue, or revenue unknown, it's due 30 days from the completed application (or from the decision on an incomplete one) and gives the specific reasons; over $1 million it's due in a reasonable time and tells them how to ask for the reasons in writing within 60 days, and a logged request makes the statement of reasons due 30 days later. The decline reason starts as a specific reason that staff edit, at most four, and "internal standards" or "didn't qualify" is refused. The letter is a PDF with the action taken, the reasons, Reg B's model ECOA notice with the federal agency, and LendHous's address and phone from Settings, which it can't go without; it's emailed to the borrower's sign-in address or marked mailed, and the borrower can open it from their loan page. The team hears when one is due, sees it in the menu and in the morning email when five days or fewer remain, and the loan shows how long its records have to be kept (12 months from the notice; 60 days over $1 million unless they asked). An owner can place a legal hold, which keeps them until it's lifted. Reopening a declined application before its notice went out drops the notice (`lib/adverse-action.ts`, `components/loan/AdverseAction.tsx`). The letter's wording, the reason texts, the federal agency and the "reasonable time" of 30 days are placeholders until the attorney sets them.
- **Payoff letters (Fla. Stat. 701.04).** The borrower asks in the portal; a title company, attorney or anyone else the borrower authorizes asks at `/payoff` with no account, attaching the borrower's signed authorization; staff log requests that came by email, fax, mail or courier with the day they were sent. Each request gets the day it counts as received (mail five business days after the postmark, anything else the day sent, a weekend or holiday moving to the next business day) and the letter's due date 10 days later, shown on `/admin/servicing`, counted in the menu and in a morning email to the team when two days or fewer remain. The borrower is told whenever someone else asks. Staff check the authorization (or, when a borrower writes from an address that isn't their sign-in, call them) before the letter can go. The letter itemizes principal, interest at the note rate, default interest, late fees and any credit held, with the total good through a date and the amount per day after; it has no wire instructions and nothing that qualifies its figures, which the statute voids. It's emailed as a PDF with a link that always opens the newest letter (`/payoff-letter/<token>`, only its hash is kept), and how sending went is kept on the letter. When a payment, reversal, fee, draw or default moves the figures, the letter is flagged and the team alerted at once, because a correction only takes the old letter's place if it arrives by 3 p.m. at least one business day before payment; the corrected letter goes the same way, with a copy to the borrower when someone else asked. A payoff paid on a letter good through that day is taken as the payoff even if the letter came up short: the mortgage is released and the rest stays owed. `/payoff-letter/demo` is a sample (`lib/payoffs.ts`, `lib/payoff-pdf.ts`, `lib/payoff-delivery.ts`). How far ahead a letter may be good through (30 days), which holidays count (the Federal Reserve's) and the letter's wording are placeholders until GT and the attorney set them.
- **The quote request.** The public site's `/start` page is a loan quote request a borrower or a broker can send. It covers the borrower (or the broker and their client), citizenship, credit band and experience; the property's address, with Google's suggestions when `GOOGLE_MAPS_API_KEY` is set, its type and, for a fix and flip or ground-up loan, its after-repair value (ARV) with up to three comps supporting it; and the loan type, purpose, exit, numbers and a funding date. The visitor sees indicative terms worked out by the same engine on the pricing the owner last published, with a clear "estimate, not an offer" note. The team gets a lead with the whole request sealed, and the leads inbox shows the address, ARV, credit band and comps (`lib/quote.ts`). `/programs` compares the four programs from the live pricing, so the advertised rates and leverage can't drift from what loans actually get (`lib/program-compare.ts`).
- **The borrower application link.** From a lead, or a loan still in review, a loan officer sends the borrower a secure link (`/apply/<token>`, no sign-in) to a five-step application. The steps are loan and contact, the property (with flood zone), the loan details, vesting and guarantors (each with date of birth, Social Security number, home address, marital status, citizenship, ownership and the declarations), and review and sign. It opens filled in with what's known, saves as they go, picks up where they left off, and works for 14 days; sending a new one turns the old one off. Signing takes four consents (placeholder wording until the attorney approves it) and the typed full legal name; the signature is kept with the time, IP address, consent version and a digest of what was signed. For a lead it starts the loan and invites the borrower to the portal; for a loan in review it updates the numbers and profile, keeping the loan's pricing. Birth dates, Social Security numbers, home addresses and the EIN stay sealed with the signed application and never enter the loan book. The loan's Credit review tab shows the application with numbers masked; revealing one takes `reveal_sensitive` and is logged (`lib/borrower-application.ts`, `lib/application-links.ts`). `/apply/demo` is a preview that sends nothing.
- **Property photos.** A borrower or contractor can send photos of the property, up to 12 per request or loan: with the quote request (the browser gets a key for two hours after sending it), on the property step of the application link (each sent as it's added, and they can take one back), and from their loan page, where the broker and the team can add them too. Each is shrunk on the phone first, checked to be a picture, and stored encrypted like every loan file. The team sees a lead's photos in the leads inbox; when the lead becomes a loan, by staff or by signing the application link, the photos move to the loan and show on the loan's Documents tab and on the borrower's and broker's loan pages. Only the team can remove one, and a partner's blind teaser never shows them (`lib/property-photos.ts`, `components/PropertyPhotoPicker.tsx`, `components/loan/PropertyPhotos.tsx`).
- **Pre-approval and proof-of-funds letters (Spec 12.3).** On the Letters page, staff with term sheet authority for the amount issue a letter to a borrower (in the portal or not): its kind, the borrower and LLC, the program, the most it covers for a purchase price and a loan, an optional property, its conditions, and how long it lasts (30 days by default, 7 to 90). It gets a number (PA-2026-0001, or PF- for proof of funds), a PDF with business-purpose and "not a commitment" wording (PLACEHOLDER until the attorney sets it), and a QR code to a public verify page that shows only whether it's genuine and still valid, whom it's for, the limits and the expiry. It's emailed to the borrower and sits on their dashboard; withdrawn, the verify page says so. The letter's details and its verify code are sealed; the page finds it by a hash of the code. A written request for one counts as a credit application under Reg B, so the screen says to send a decline notice when you won't issue one (`lib/letters.ts`, `lib/letter-store.ts`, `lib/letter-pdf.ts`, `app/verify`).
- **Partners' and buyers' money (Spec P7–P9, 8.6).** When LendHous accepts a partner's bid, the loan records the partner's share. Once the loan is clear to close and the title company's escrow account is verified by a call-back, the winning partner sees where to wire its share on its loan page (never by email) and says when it's sent; the team marks it received on the Closing tab, and the loan can't fund until it has. Partners have a **My portfolio** page and buyers the portfolio they had: each loan's share, rate, balance, draws, what's been paid to them after servicing, and whether it's late. Every investor's share of each payment comes from the servicing ledger: interest at the loan's rate, less the servicing fee LendHous keeps (the difference to the partner's rate, or a buyer's 1% strip), plus principal and payoffs; amortizing loans split by the schedule; late fees stay with LendHous (PLACEHOLDER until the agreements say). A nightly job makes each partner's and buyer's statement on the 1st for the month before, keeps it sealed exactly as made, and emails their people a link; they download it as a PDF or a spreadsheet, and the team can open any of them. Demo seats see a sample (`lib/partner-funding.ts`, `lib/investor-statements.ts`, `lib/statement-store.ts`, `lib/statement-pdf.ts`).
- **Tax forms.** Partners, loan buyers and brokers sign a W-9 (or a W-8BEN or W-8BEN-E if they aren't U.S. persons) on their Tax form page (`/admin/account/tax`); the taxpayer ID and the signature are encrypted, and only the last four digits are ever shown. The owners' Tax forms page (`/admin/tax`) lists each year's forms: 1099-INT for partners' and buyers' interest after servicing ($10 or more; corporations, IRAs and tax-exempt payees are exempt), 1099-NEC for broker fees in the year they're paid ($2,000 or more from 2026; corporations exempt), and 1098 for $600 or more of interest from an individual borrower on a loan secured by real property, business-purpose loans included. It shows who has no W-9 (24% backup withholding until they sign one) and the deadlines moved off weekends (recipients' copies and the 1099-NEC by Feb 1, 2027 for 2026; e-filing by March 31). An owner with `reveal_sensitive` exports the year as a CSV for IRIS, the IRS's only e-file system from 2027; every export is in the audit log. The thresholds and exemptions are PLACEHOLDER for the CPA to confirm (`TAX_CONFIG` in `lib/tax-forms.ts`), and so is the substitute W-9's wording.
- **Repeat borrowers.** A borrower's LendHous loans funded in the last 24 months and paid as agreed count toward the next loan's price, counted when it's submitted and kept with it: the highest tier reached takes rate and points off (PLACEHOLDER tiers at `/admin/pricing`: 1 loan 0.25%, 3 loans 0.50% and 0.25 points, 6 loans 0.75% and 0.50, 10 loans 1.00% and 0.75), and a late payment (one whose late fee wasn't waived) or a default in that time pauses it. Staff's old "repeat borrower" mark now means one loan from before this system, and borrowers and brokers can't set it. From a funded or paid-off loan the borrower starts a "New loan on another property": the application opens with their last signed one's entity, guarantors and past deals, and asks for Social Security numbers, birth dates, credit scores and the credit questions again. Papers accepted on an earlier loan carry over as accepted while they're still good (the entity's articles and EIN letter for the same entity, good standing for 90 days, IDs for a year when the guarantors are the same: PLACEHOLDER, `CARRY_OVER` in `lib/repeat-borrower.ts`), past deals already confirmed stay confirmed, and staff can ask again for anything carried over (`lib/repeat-borrower.ts`).
- **The broker program.** A brokerage signs LendHous's broker agreement in its portal (PLACEHOLDER wording: business-purpose loans only, accurate submissions, every borrower-paid fee disclosed, non-circumvention with a 12-month borrower protection, no authority to bind LendHous, indemnity) with its owners and officers, NMLS ID or state license if it has one, and its articles and good standing. The firm and its owners are screened against the OFAC list, and an owner approves it on the Brokers page (`/admin/brokers`, the new `manage_brokers` permission), giving a reason when a name was close to the list; until then it can't send deals. Each broker's fee is set in points before the term sheet, shown on it, tracked from expected to unpaid to paid, and counted toward the 1099-NEC in the year it's paid. An approved broker shares a referral link (`/start?ref=…`): the quote request and the loan it becomes are the broker's, and so is that borrower's next loan within 12 months. Brokers download a quote PDF for their client with their name on it that still names LendHous as the lender and says it isn't a commitment, and the reports count loans, referrals and fees by broker (`lib/brokers.ts`). Term sheets now count points spread over the term toward Florida's usury cap (18% up to $500,000, Fla. Stat. 687.03): over it takes an owner's approval, a broker's fee that would take it over is flagged for the attorney, and over 25% can't go out (`lib/usury.ts`).
- **Phones first.** On a phone the borrower's loan page leads with the loan at a glance (amount, rate and closing, or balance, next payment and maturity), shows the timeline as one line with every step a tap away, and folds a scope of work once it's sent. The application hides the tab bar, keeps Back and Next at the foot of the screen, and starts the first question sooner. The staff pipeline puts each queue item's deadline or clock under its name and folds the counts by kind and the filters behind a tap; loan headers show the property as a thumbnail beside the address. Staff get a tab bar too (Pipeline, Leads, Draws and Servicing with what's waiting, and Menu), and a loan's tabs are one row that stays under the top bar and slides sideways; opening one from far down starts it at its top. The quote asks the kind of loan first, as tiles, puts city and state, ZIP and county, and beds, baths and square feet two or three to a row, and keeps See my terms at the foot of the screen; the other public pages show See your terms there once the first screen is passed. Every field is at least 16px on a phone, so iPhones don't zoom in on it, and buttons answer a press at once. A funded loan shows its money before its paperwork, folds its documents to one line once nothing is left to send, and drops the agents card; an agent not on file is one Add button. A borrower's Upload tab, and Upload now on the home page, open one sheet of everything still needed across their loans, each a tap from the camera or their files and marked sent once it's in; on the loan page a document shows Sending… while it goes and a line says when it's in. Pop-ups rise from the foot of a phone's screen. The draws page shows each draw's amount and buttons and folds its lines, photos and lien waivers behind a tap. Partners, buyers and brokers get a tab bar of their own pages, and a broker's phone shares the referral link through its own share menu. Saving the quote's terms also stays at the foot of the screen. With no signal the portal says it's offline, before a save or upload fails. Text meets WCAG AA contrast (the faint grey is #686F69, 4.5:1 or better on every surface), every control has a name, headings run in order, chart bars a keyboard reaches say their month and amount, and on a touch screen buttons are 44px and small text links become 44px buttons; `npm run ux:audit` checks it. Added to a phone's home screen the portal opens full screen, and each portal's home offers once to put it there (one tap on Android, Safari's Share menu on an iPhone). Desktops keep everything open.
- **The borrower's documents and agents.** A borrower's loan page lists its documents in two tabs: To do (anything sent back first, then what's needed, then what only applies to some loans) and Submitted, each with a plain badge (Action needed, Needed, If it applies, Uploaded, In review, Accepted). When the team sends one back it writes what it still needs, which the borrower sees on the document with an "Upload a corrected version" button. A borrower can add a document nobody asked for ("Have something we didn't ask for?"); it goes to the team marked as added by the borrower. From the accepted term sheet until closing, the page asks once a sign-in for their title or escrow agent and their insurance agent (a name or company, and an email or phone) until both are on file, and they can change them under Your agents; the team sees them on the Closing tab and fills the title agent's details from the borrower's with one tap. My loans splits active and closed loans, and each loan card says how many items are still needed with a link to send them (`lib/borrower-docs.ts`, `components/loan/BorrowerDocuments.tsx`, `components/loan/YourAgents.tsx`).
- **The design pass.** Every loan leads with its property: the first property photo, else a Google Street View picture fetched by the server with the same `GOOGLE_MAPS_API_KEY` (the Street View Static API has to be enabled on that key; the key never reaches a browser), else a line drawing of the loan's program in the brand's sage. Partners' blind teasers only ever get the drawing. Loan pages show the loan's journey from its stage history: each step's date and how long it took, today's step, the target closing, then draws and maturity (`lib/loan-timeline.ts`). A borrower's loan page and loan card show one next step, from one rule (`lib/next-step.ts`), and every screen uses the same sentence-case status words (`lib/status-words.ts`) and written-out dates, with no "full-boat" or "LTARV" on borrower screens. Partners and buyers see what was paid to them each month for a year and when their money comes back (`lib/portfolio-charts.ts`); a funded rehab loan shows its budget paid out line by line. On a phone, borrowers get a tab bar (My loans, Upload, Messages, Account). The staff menu folds the owners' tools under Admin and keeps theme, security and sign-out in the account menu, so it fits a laptop's screen. The home page shows a drawing of a South Florida house (PLACEHOLDER until LendHous picks a licensed photograph) and, once borrowers opt in from a funded loan, a "Recently funded" list with the city, program, rounded amount and their photo, never a name, company or street address; the team can take one down (`lib/showcase.ts`, `/api/showcase/<loan>`).
- **Instant quote, terms first.** The quote at `/start` asks for the deal (address, program, numbers, a credit and experience band) and shows indicative terms at once, worked out in the visitor's browser from the published pricing by the same engine the loan would use: the most it could lend, the rate and its range, points, the term, the monthly payment, and the cash to close, itemized (down payment, points, lender fees and closing costs at a PLACEHOLDER 2%). No name, email or phone is asked until the visitor saves the terms, and nothing about them is kept until then; the page only counts, anonymously, that someone started a quote and saw terms (`QuoteCount`). Saving sends the request as before. A borrower who then makes an account with the same email starts their first application from the quote. The same quote is a widget at `/embed/quote` for other sites to frame (`?ref=` a broker's referral code, `?site=` the site's name): brokers copy its code from their page and owners from Settings for GT's wholesale and realty sites, and it grows to fit its frame. Only `/embed/*` may be framed; every other page refuses (X-Frame-Options and `frame-ancestors`). Reports show the funnel by channel and referring broker: started, saw terms, saved, became loans, funded (`lib/quote.ts`, `lib/quote-funnel.ts`).
- **The maturity desk and rental refinances.** Every funded fix and flip, bridge or construction loan carries the borrower's exit plan: selling (with the list price and the expected closing), refinancing into a LendHous rental loan, refinancing elsewhere, an extension, or paying it off another way, and how sure they are. Borrowers can say any time on their loan page; the nightly servicing check asks them at 120, 90, 60 and 30 days before maturity (PLACEHOLDER days, `EXIT_OUTREACH_DAYS`), by its own email or added to the 60- and 30-day maturity notice, and it's their next step until they answer. With no answer at 60 days it goes in the team's work queue. Choosing a LendHous rental loan starts a DSCR application from the loan: the same property at its value after the work, their entity, guarantors and past deals, and the finished rehab as a project. When that loan funds, its money pays off the old one for what it owes that day, recorded as the old loan's payoff. Servicing has a maturity desk of the short-term loans by how soon they come due, each with its plan, and the reports show plans given, refinances kept and extensions signed (`lib/exit-plans.ts`).
- **Borrower statements and money (Spec 8.6).** On the 1st, the nightly `statements` job also makes a statement for every funded loan for the month before (and the paid-off month's last one), kept sealed as made and emailed to the borrower with a link: the principal balance, the rate and how interest is charged, maturity, payments received and billed in the month, late fees, the rehab holdback drawn and left, what's due next and anything past due, an estimated payoff with its daily amount, and the interest paid so far this year (`lib/borrower-statements.ts`, `lib/borrower-statement-pdf.ts`, `/api/borrower-statements/<id>`). A funded loan's page shows how to pay (LendHous's receiving instructions, set in Settings, with a reminder to confirm them by phone), its statements as PDFs, and the interest paid this year and last, with whether a 1098 comes for it; what's due, the history and the payoff letter are just below. The team sees a loan's statements on its Servicing tab. `npm run statements -- 2026-10` makes a missed month by hand.
- **A work queue with clocks.** The pipeline opens on each person's queue: the things waiting on the team one by one (a payoff letter, a wire to verify, a draw, documents to review, a term sheet to send), their own loans first and then what the team could pick up, each linked to the loan's tab where it's done (`?tab=`). Legal deadlines come first, then items past their target, then nearly there. Targets are PLACEHOLDER until an owner sets them in Settings: a new application looked at within 4 business hours, a complete application to a term sheet in 1 business day, a draw decided within 24 hours, a document reviewed within 1 business day. Business hours are 9 to 5 Eastern on weekdays, less Florida's paid state holidays (Fla. Stat. 110.117). Items turn amber at three quarters of the target and red past it, and red ones go in the owners' morning email with whose loan it is. The counts by kind stay below as a compact list. The pipeline also shows as a board of columns by stage, where dragging a card only does what the loan's next step allows. Reports show the median and slowest waits against the targets, overall and by loan officer (`lib/sla.ts`, `lib/work-queue.ts`, `lib/board.ts`, `lib/speed.ts`).

It starts from the matrix on the loan's own pricing version, and they set the rate, points, term and prepayment terms, seeing the loan amount, payment, cash to close and all-in cost as they go. The underwriter's dollar limit applies. A rate or points outside the matrix's range for that loan, an all-in cost at or over the ceiling, or a rate over 18% on a loan of $500,000 or less (Florida's usury cap) needs an exception: the underwriter says why, the owners are told, and an owner other than whoever asked approves or declines it, which is recorded on the term sheet. Nothing can go above 25% (criminal usury). Each term sheet goes out as a numbered version, emailed to the borrower and broker, with a PDF made on the server, and is open for 7 days. The borrower sees only the newest one, without the matrix or the exception, and accepts it by typing their name, asks for changes (back to underwriting, with their note), or declines it (which withdraws the application). Accepting locks the rate and points: later number edits and pricing changes don't move them. A nightly `termSheets` job reminds the borrower and broker two days before one expires, and expires it the day after its last day, sending the loan back to underwriting. Staff see every version and open any version's PDF (`lib/term-sheets.ts`, `lib/term-sheet-pdf.ts`, `components/loan/TermSheetBuilder.tsx`). The 7 days, the prepayment options and the term sheet's wording are placeholders until GT and the attorney set them.
- **Pricing you can change safely.** Every loan is priced on a pricing version and keeps it: its rate, points, leverage, late fee, default rate and extension terms come from the version it was quoted on, so publishing new numbers never moves a loan already priced. The owner edits rates, points, leverage, minimum scores, terms, loan sizes, fees and the note's late-payment and extension terms at `/admin/pricing`. They see what the change does to each application still in review before publishing it with a reason, and every version is kept and audited. Rates above 25% a year (criminal usury in Florida) can't be published, and anything above 18% is flagged for the attorney. New applications get the current version; staff can move an application still in review onto it with **Reprice on current pricing**. Loans saved before versions existed stay on the original numbers in `lib/pricing-config.ts` (`lib/pricing-versions.ts`, `lib/pricing-store.ts`).
- **The backend.** Sign-in, roles and permissions, encryption at rest, dual control, audit, storage, mail, jobs, health, and the release guard. The loan book is saved as JSON records (`BookRecord`), one per loan, bid, draw and so on, with the rules in `lib/book.ts`. Splitting those records into their own tables is still open, for when reporting across them needs it.

## Run

You need Node 22+ and a MongoDB replica set: Prisma needs one for transactions. Use either:

```bash
docker compose up -d          # mongo:7, one-node replica set (same as UPB)
npm run db:dev                # or: the same without Docker, data in .mongo-data/ (Ctrl-C to stop)
```

Then:

```bash
cp .env.example .env          # local defaults work as is; set CRON_SECRET to try /api/cron
npm install                   # postinstall runs prisma generate
npm run db:seed               # drops the local database, syncs validators and indexes, adds the demo seats
npm run dev                   # http://localhost:3000 is the public site; Portal login opens /login
```

`npm run db:indexes` syncs the MongoDB validators and indexes. It is safe to run again; the seed and release builds both run it. There are no migrations. As in UPB, never use `prisma db push`.

## What is encrypted

With `FIELD_ENCRYPTION_KEYS`, before anything is stored:

- every loan book record: names, addresses, credit scores, liquidity, messages, draw contacts and photo requests
- a website brief's name, email, phone, city, state and notes
- saved alerts in the notifications inbox
- every stored file and its name, so the bucket only ever holds ciphertext

Sign-in email addresses, account names and phone numbers are not, because sign-in and alerts look people up by them. The audit log holds who did what and from where, not the details. Passwords are hashed, and password, photo, application, title and payoff links are stored only as hashes. Files always come through the signed-in route rather than a signed link to the bucket, since the bucket can't decrypt them.

Records saved before this still read. `npm run db:encrypt` and the daily job encrypt them, and move records to the newest key after a rotation. To rotate, put the new key first (`k2:...,k1:...`), run `db:encrypt`, and only then remove the old key.

## MongoDB instead of PostgreSQL

Spec v3 assumes PostgreSQL. Lendhous uses MongoDB to match UPB, and covers the gaps this way:

- **No row-level security.** Every server action checks `can()` from `lib/permissions.ts`, and from B1 every loan query goes through one data layer scoped to the viewer.
- **No CHECK constraints.** Collection validators in `lib/mongo-validators.ts`, applied by `db:indexes`, reject rule-breaking writes at the database. Dual approval is the first one.
- **No migrations.** Schema changes need a backfill script under `scripts/` when existing documents must change.
- **32-bit `Int`.** Money is integer cents. Loan-level amounts fit (cap $21.4M). Capital totals will use `BigInt` when they arrive (B3).
- **Append-only ledgers (B4).** An Atlas custom role without update or delete rights on the ledger collections, used through a second connection.
